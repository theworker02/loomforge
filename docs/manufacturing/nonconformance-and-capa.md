# Nonconformance, rework, and corrective-action procedure — M1

**Document:** LF-QA-002 · **Revision:** A0 (proposed)

## Immediate containment

1. Stop the affected operation and place the unit, part, lot, or evidence in a clearly identified hold state.
2. Preserve terminal/housing samples, force traces, test data, photos, recipe, fixture ID, and software version. Do not overwrite a failed job.
3. Create an NCR with unique ID, date, reporter, configuration, observed condition, and affected serial numbers.
4. Escalate immediately for any guard/interlock/E-stop issue, unexpected motion, connector mismatch, sensor calibration issue, unexplained electrical result, or material damage.

## Disposition options

| Disposition | Permitted use |
|---|---|
| Rework to baseline | Controlled instructions and reinspection are available |
| Repair / use-as-is under deviation | Manufacturer-authorized engineering disposition; risk and verification recorded |
| Scrap / retain for analysis | Unsafe, damaged, or diagnostic material; preserve evidence as needed |
| Return to supplier | Receiving defect supported by supplier agreement |

No one may use “adjust and retry” as a disposition for a force, seating, guard, or electrical anomaly without documenting why the retry is safe and what evidence it will generate.

## CAPA trigger

Open corrective action when a critical issue occurs once, the same noncritical issue repeats twice, a supplier defect recurs, or an issue affects more than one M1 unit. CAPA records problem statement, containment, root-cause method, correction, preventive action, verification of effectiveness, owner, and closure authority.
